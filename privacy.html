Log My Drives Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026

Overview

Log My Drives is a free teen driver permit hours tracker. This policy explains what the app stores, where it is stored, who can see it, and how to delete it.

In short:

  • Drives are saved on your phone and synced through Apple's iCloud so the teen and supervisors see the same log.
  • Synced drives, including a simplified map of each route, are stored in a shared iCloud database. Anyone who has the teen's invite code can see that family's drives. Share the code only with your supervisors.
  • The app has no ads, no analytics and no third-party tracking.
  • "Delete All Data" in the app does not remove drives from iCloud. Email us to have them removed.

Data Storage

On your phone

The app saves your profile, family, drives, full GPS routes and achievements on your iPhone using Apple's SwiftData framework.

In iCloud (shared with your family)

When you are signed in to iCloud, the app syncs your family's data through the public database of Apple's iCloud service (CloudKit). This is how a supervisor's phone gets the teen's drives. The app stores three kinds of records there:

  • Family record: the teen's name, the teen's state and the family's invite code.
  • Supervisor records: each supervisor's name and role (for example, parent), the invite code they joined with, and a random member ID.
  • Drive records: start and end times, total, day, night and bad-weather time, distance, road types and weather tags, the state, the supervisor's name and role, any supervisor notes, approval status and who approved it, whether the drive counts as instructor time, a change log (what was changed, when, and the name of the person who changed it), and a simplified route. See Location Data.

iCloud also attaches an ID to each record that identifies the iCloud account that created it. This ID is specific to this app and does not include your Apple ID email address or your name.

If you are not signed in to iCloud, nothing is uploaded and your drives stay only on your phone. They also won't appear on your supervisors' phones.

Who can see the shared records

The public database is not private to your family, and these records are not end-to-end encrypted. The app looks up a family's records by the teen's invite code, so anyone who has the invite code can see that family's drives, including routes, names and supervisor notes. Treat the invite code like a password and give it only to the supervisors you choose.

We (the developer) can also view these records using Apple's CloudKit tools. We use them only to run, support and fix the app, and to count how many families and supervisors use it (by state and role, without names).

In iCloud Keychain

Your name, role, state, invite codes and member ID are saved in iCloud Keychain. This lets you set up a new iPhone signed in to the same Apple ID and keep your profile and drives. Apple syncs these items across your own devices. We cannot see them.

In your private iCloud database

The app also keeps one account-recovery record in your private iCloud database. It holds the same items as the Keychain copy: name, role, state, invite codes and member ID. It lets you get your account back on a new phone even if iCloud Keychain is off. Only your Apple ID can read your private database. We cannot.

Backups (being set up)

We are setting up a nightly backup of the shared iCloud records described above (family, supervisor and drive records), so lost or damaged data can be restored. Once it is running:

  • Each backup copies every field, including names, supervisor notes and routes.
  • Each backup is encrypted before it is stored, and only we hold the key to decrypt it.
  • Backups are stored privately with GitHub, the service we use to run the backup, and each one is deleted automatically after 90 days.
  • This means a family's data can remain in backups for up to 90 days after it is removed from iCloud.

iCloud is operated by Apple. Apple's privacy policy covers how Apple stores and protects data in iCloud. We do not run our own servers for app data.

Location Data

The app uses your phone's GPS to record your route while a drive is in progress. Location data is:

  • Recorded only while you are actively logging a drive. If you leave the app during a drive, it keeps recording, and iOS shows the location indicator until the drive ends.
  • Kept in full detail only on your phone.
  • Uploaded to the shared iCloud database in simplified form after the drive ends. The route is reduced to at most about 240 points for each part of a drive (a new part starts whenever the road type, weather or day/night changes), with each point accurate to about 1 meter.
  • Visible to anyone with the family's invite code, as described above.
  • Never shared live. Supervisors cannot see your location while you drive.

Maps in the app and in PDF exports are drawn by Apple Maps, which loads map images from Apple for the area shown.

Data We Do Not Collect

Log My Drives does not collect:

  • Email addresses, phone numbers or other contact information
  • Your Apple ID email address or password
  • Advertising IDs or cross-app tracking identifiers
  • Analytics or usage data. The app contains no analytics service, and it does not send usage data to us or anyone else.
  • Your location outside of an active drive

The app contains no ads and no third-party code that collects data.

No Separate Account

Log My Drives does not ask for an email address or password, and you don't create an account with us. The app syncs using the iCloud account already signed in on your iPhone. During setup you enter a display name, which is shared with your family as described above. A first name or nickname works fine.

Children's Privacy

Log My Drives is designed for teen drivers (typically ages 14–17) and their parents or other supervising adults. It is not intended for children under 13.

The app does collect information about teens: the name they enter, their state, their drive times and conditions, and simplified routes. When the teen is signed in to iCloud, this is synced through the shared iCloud database and can be seen by anyone with the family's invite code. We recommend that a parent help set up the app and decide who receives the invite code.

A parent or guardian can ask us to delete a teen's synced data at any time. See Data Deletion.

Data Deletion

Deleting one drive removes it from your phone and your family's phones. In iCloud, the drive record is marked as deleted so other phones remove it, but the record and its contents are kept.

"Delete All Data" in Settings:

  • Removes all drives, routes, achievements, profiles and family data from your phone.
  • Removes your profile and invite codes from iCloud Keychain, which also removes them from your other devices.
  • Deletes your account-recovery record from your private iCloud database.
  • If you are the teen, deletes the family record (teen name, state and invite code) from the shared iCloud database.

"Delete All Data" does not delete drive records or supervisor records from the shared iCloud database. They stay there and can still be found with the invite code. Removing a supervisor from a family also marks their record as removed rather than deleting it.

To have a family's drive and supervisor records deleted from iCloud, email us at busselle@gmail.com with the teen's invite code, and we will delete them. Once nightly backups are running, copies can remain in backups for up to 90 days after that.

Deleting the app from your phone removes only the data on that phone.
